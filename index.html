Abstract

A risk field that forecasts, not just reacts.

Safety assessment of autonomous vehicles needs risk representations that are predictive, temporally coherent, interpretable, and able to answer what-if questions about the ego's own decisions. We propose an ego-centric risk field built on world-model-conditioned normalizing flows: a deterministic latent state rolled over the horizon conditions an invertible flow, giving an exact, grid-evaluable occupancy density without sampling.

The field couples two flows, a single-target model for the ego's occupancy and a scene-level autoregressive joint for the surrounding agents conditioned on the ego, both conditioned on a bird's-eye map of the road. Per-cell risk is the ego occupancy × an oriented bounding-box meeting probability × a physically grounded kinetic-energy severity, aggregated over agents into a horizon-indexed risk tensor. Evaluated on InD intersections, the congested-highway AD4CHE dataset, and rounD roundabouts under an assumption-free post-encroachment-time conflict label, the field detects conflicts competitively-to-better than kinematic and occupancy-overlap baselines, with the largest margins in congested and roundabout traffic, and warns earlier. It also supports a physically grounded counterfactual over candidate ego maneuvers, grounded in mechanics rather than a learned reaction, that fixed-plan occupancy methods structurally lack.

Method

Two world-model-conditioned flows, one risk field.

1
Exact occupancy. A normalizing flow conditioned on the rolled-out latent gives a grid-evaluable density per future step, with no sampling or trajectory stitching.
2
Ego × others, coupled. A single-target flow models the ego's occupancy; a scene-level autoregressive joint models the surrounding agents conditioned on the ego. Both read a BEV road map.
3
Physically grounded risk. Per cell: ego occupancy × oriented-box meeting probability × kinetic-energy severity (½μ ‖Δv‖²), aggregated into a horizon-indexed risk tensor.

Counterfactual

What if the ego brakes?

The same AD4CHE conflict (an ego truck closing on a slower truck), re-scored under three candidate ego maneuvers on the identical colorbar. Because the severity is collision kinetic energy, braking lowers it by the laws of mechanics — the risk field visibly shrinks under braking and grows under acceleration. Averaged over 20 test scenes, braking cuts the aggregated risk by 12.7% and accelerating raises it by 15.5% — a causal what-if query that fixed-plan occupancy methods structurally cannot answer.

Our field wins both AUROC and AP on the congested-highway and roundabout regimes — exactly where constant-velocity kinematics break down — and beats the same-backbone occupancy baseline (PORA) everywhere. On smooth, low-speed InD intersections, motion is so constant-velocity-predictable that a kinematic TTC baseline is near-optimal; our field stays competitive there and gives the best early warning. And unlike any baseline, it also yields a probabilistic field, a physical severity, an early-warning lead, and a physically grounded counterfactual.
